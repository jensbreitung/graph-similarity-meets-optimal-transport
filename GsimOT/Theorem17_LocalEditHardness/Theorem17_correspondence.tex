\begin{center}{\Large\bfseries Theorem 16: inapproximability of the optimal-transport local edit distance}\\[4pt]

{\small Correspondence between the paper and the Lean~4/Mathlib formalisation}\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{Paper.} J.~Breitung, T.~Gervens, M.~Grohe. \emph{Graph Similarity Meets Optimal Transport} (file \texttt{Graph\_Similarity\_Meets\_Optimal\_Transport.pdf} in the repository). Statement, equation, page and line numbers refer to this PDF; \texttt{docs/NUMBERING.md} lists the Lean files and declarations of all numbered statements.\par\smallskip

The folder \texttt{GsimOT/Theorem17\_LocalEditHardness} formalises the reduction of Theorem~16 (p.~12, proof in App.~G, pp.~51--52) from Three-Partition. It gives the case $p=1$ of Theorem~4\,(2). Shared notions come from \texttt{GsimOT/Common}; they are listed at the end.\par\paragraph{Checking.} Run \texttt{bash build.sh} in the repository root (see \texttt{README.md}). The script builds the library and runs \texttt{scripts/CheckAxioms.lean}, which must report only the standard axioms \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, \texttt{Quot.sound}: no \texttt{sorry} and no additional axioms.

\paragraph{Main statement in Lean.} For an instance $(A,a_1,\dots,a_{3m})$ of Three-Partition, let $F_1$ be the union of paths on $a_i$
vertices and $F_2$ the union of $m$ cycles on $A$ vertices.
\lean{GsimOT.Hardness.ThreePartition.dOTOne_le_of_isYes}: for a YES-instance with all $a_i\ge2$,
$d^{\mathrm{OT}}_1(F_1,F_2)\le1$.
\lean{GsimOT.Hardness.ThreePartition.two_le_dOTOne_of_not_isYes}: for a NO-instance,
$d^{\mathrm{OT}}_1(F_1,F_2)\ge2$.

\section*{Notes on the formalisation}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item Only the correctness of the reduction is formalised: for the constructed graphs, YES-instances have a small and NO-instances a large distance. The complexity side (polynomial running time, NP-hardness of Three-Partition, H\aa{}stad's bound for MaxCut) is not formalised. The results of Section~4 and their shared gadgets are in the namespace \lean{GsimOT.Hardness}.
\item $d^{\mathrm{OT}}_1$ is \lean{GsimOT.dOTOne}, the definition of Proposition~8 and Theorem~9. By Eq.~(7) (\lean{GsimOT.dOT_one_one_eq_dOTOne}) it equals the operator-norm distance $d^{\mathrm{OT}}_{1\to1}$. The paper bounds $d^{\bullet}_1$ via permutations of blowups and then applies Theorem~1 (p.~52, l.~1667). The proof works directly with doubly stochastic matrices, with the same case distinction: a row of $Q$ split over two cycles (Case~2) or adjacent vertices sent to different cycles (Case~1) gives a local cost $\ge2$, and otherwise the counting after Eq.~(44) gives a solution.
\item The formalisation assumes $a_i\ge2$ for the YES-direction (\lean{GsimOT.Hardness.ThreePartition.dOTOne_le_of_isYes}); the NO-direction has no hypothesis on the $a_i$.
\end{enumerate}
