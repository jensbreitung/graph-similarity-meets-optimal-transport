{\small Correspondence between the paper and the Lean~4/Mathlib formalisation}\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{Paper.} J.~Breitung, T.~Gervens, M.~Grohe. \emph{Graph Similarity Meets Optimal Transport} (file \texttt{Graph\_Similarity\_Meets\_Optimal\_Transport.pdf} in the repository). Statement, equation, page and line numbers refer to this PDF; \texttt{docs/NUMBERING.md} lists the Lean files and declarations of all numbered statements.\par\smallskip

The folder \texttt{GsimOT/Common} contains the notions shared by all formalised results (Sections~1--2 and App.~A of the paper) together with the auxiliary results that are used by more than one proof: Observation~21, Lemma~22, Lemma~29 and Eq.~(3), the $\ell^p$/operator norms with Lemma~19, the cut norm, the optimal transport distances and the attainment of their minima (App.~A), the edit distance with Eq.~(7), and the graphs of the reductions of Section~4. A reviewer-oriented overview of all definitions is \texttt{Common\_overview.pdf}.\par\paragraph{Checking.} Run \texttt{bash build.sh} in the repository root (see \texttt{README.md}). The script builds the library and runs \texttt{scripts/CheckAxioms.lean}, which must report only the standard axioms \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, \texttt{Quot.sound}: no \texttt{sorry} and no additional axioms.

\section*{Notes on the formalisation}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item The paper defines $C_1(Q)$ as the operator $1\to1$-norm of $B_{1\to1}(G,H,Q)$ and gives the formula $C_1(Q)=\max_{(v,w)\in\mathrm{supp}(Q)}L_Q(v,w)$ (Sec.~2, p.~5, l.~184--189; Eq.~(7), p.~14). The formalisation takes this formula as the definition of \lean{costOne} and proves the identity (\lean{opNorm_Bpq_one_one_eq_costOne}).
\item $d_1^{\mathrm{OT}}$, $d^{\mathrm{OT}}_{\mathrm{ed}}$, $d^{\mathrm{OT}}_\square$, $d^{\mathrm{OT}}_{p\to q}$ and $\delta^{\mathrm{OT}}_{p\to q}$ are defined as \emph{infima}. The paper takes minima and argues that they are attained (App.~A, pp.~14--15, l.~503--510). This is formalised for the operator norms (\lean{exists_deltaOTPQ_eq}, \lean{exists_dOT_eq}, following the paper's lower-semicontinuity argument; used by Claim~20) and for the edit distance (\lean{exists_dOTEdit_eq}, used by Theorem~15). For bijections the minimum over the finite set $\mathrm{Bij}(V,W)$ is the infimum.
\item Normalisation: for graphs of the same order $n$, $\delta_\diamondsuit=d_\diamondsuit/s(n)$ and $\delta^{\mathrm{OT}}_\diamondsuit=d^{\mathrm{OT}}_\diamondsuit/s(n)$ with $s(n)=n^2$ for the edit and the cut distance (p.~1, l.~24--27) and $s(n)=n^{\kappa_{p\to q}}$, $\kappa_{p\to q}=1/q+1-1/p$, for $\|\cdot\|_{p\to q}$ (p.~3, l.~88--90; App.~B, p.~15, l.~513--514), so $s(n)=n$ for $\|\cdot\|_1$. In Lean, \lean{deltaPQ} multiplies $d_{p\to q}$ by $n^{-\kappa}$, the other normalised distances divide by $n$ or $n^2$, and \lean{deltaOTPQ_eq_dOT} proves $\delta^{\mathrm{OT}}_{p\to q}=n^{-\kappa}d^{\mathrm{OT}}_{p\to q}$.
\item Lemma~22 is stated in the paper for $\diamondsuit\in\{\mathrm{ed},1\}$; both cases are formalised, $\diamondsuit=1$ in \texttt{Lemma27\_LockingMarkers.lean} and $\diamondsuit=\mathrm{ed}$ in \texttt{Lemma27\_LockingMarkersEdit.lean}. In Lean, the clique vertex $y_{i+1}$ is \texttt{Sum.inr i} and the marker index $j$ of the paper is \texttt{j.val + 1}.
\item Lemma~29 is proved in the paper for an optimal $Q$ and $L=d_1^{\mathrm{OT}}(G,H)$. Its proof only uses that all supported local costs of $Q$ are at most $L$, which is how it is stated in Lean. It is shared by Proposition~8 (via Lemma~27) and Theorem~9.
\item Eq.~(3): \lean{exists_birkhoff_decomposition} sums over all permutations with weights $\ge0$; \lean{exists_birkhoff_decomposition_pos} is the exact statement with $\lambda_i>0$.
\end{enumerate}
